01

The Brief That Became a Business

Ecowell had no physical brand presence when I joined. No website, no packaging in production, nothing customer-facing — what existed lived on paper. The founder's original ask was narrow: help with social media creatives and packaging support.

Within a month, that scope changed. As the founder saw the design decisions I was making, the ask expanded into building the company's entire design language from the ground up, across a portfolio that would eventually span seven nutraceutical categories: plant protein, diabetes care, collagen, shilajit, performance coffee, stamina boosters, and functional supplement sticks.

That created a compounding design problem. Each category serves a fundamentally different buyer with a different trust trigger:

  • DiabeVita buyers are evaluating clinical credibility and medical trust.
  • EcoVita Plant Protein buyers are evaluating performance and vitality.
  • Shilajit Resin buyers are evaluating authenticity and heritage.
  • Skin Power Collagen buyers are evaluating premium beauty cues.

Designing each in isolation risked fragmentation. Designing one generic system risked converting in none of them — because it would ignore what each specific buyer needs to feel before they trust the product.

The Real Challenge

Build a full brand system with zero existing infrastructure, for a founder who initially hired me for a much smaller job, with no internal sales history to lean on.

02

Research & Discovery

There was nothing live to audit — no website, no packaging in production. Two packaging concepts existed on paper, and they read like a generic, unbranded local product: no trust cues, and no real sense of who they were actually speaking to. Beyond that, just a founder's instinct that the brand needed to feel more premium and trustworthy than typical category players.

Competitive Research

I studied six category leaders and adjacent Amazon sellers in depth — their websites, their listings, and their full creative ecosystems — to understand what was actually working, and just as importantly, what to leave behind.

Customer Signal

With no historical sales data available in a pure startup phase, I relied on direct customer feedback as it came in. Two early signals mattered most: zero complaints about product quality, and an emerging base of repeat customers — meaning the product was already earning trust, and design's job was to make that trust visible as fast as possible.

On the Ground

I accompanied the founder on multiple visits to printing companies across Delhi, including one of the largest in India, to determine what packaging quality and finish were realistically achievable within budget — keeping every design decision grounded in what could actually be manufactured well.

03

The Solution: Unity Without Uniformity

Rather than working off a fixed brief, I presented the founder with three candidate directions:

  • Bold-clinical — a high-contrast, Western supplement look.
  • Earthy-heritage — Ayurveda-forward, warm and traditional.
  • Minimal-premium — sleek, informative, restrained.

We aligned on minimal-premium — not because it looked prettier, but because it was the only direction that could sit credibly next to Kapiva and imported supplements on Amazon, scale across diabetes care, collagen, protein, and immunity without visual chaos, and communicate trust to a mature buyer and aspiration to a younger one at the same time.

The principle behind the resulting system is Unity Without Uniformity — a two-layer brand architecture, built from scratch with no existing equity to preserve or work around, governed by four rules:

  1. Minimal, never empty — every surface earns its space.
  2. Premium through restraint — no gradients, no gloss, no shouting.
  3. Informative first, decorative second — the customer needs to know, not be dazzled.
  4. Category-flexible, brand-consistent — every SKU is distinctly itself, unmistakably Ecowell.

Part of the role was steering the founder away from unrealistic, internet-trend-driven packaging ideas that don't hold up in the real market and are too expensive to produce at scale — prioritizing what's manufacturable and what actually reads well on shelf and on screen over what merely looks impressive in a mockup.

04

Execution

A modern brand lives in multiple environments. Every touchpoint was engineered for its specific context.

4.1 — PACKAGING & PRINT PRODUCTION

Designed 20+ print-ready packaging artworks across jars, boxes, pouches, sachets, cylinders, and labels, including hands-on sourcing decisions made directly with printing vendors in Delhi. Material finishes were used strategically — matte for clinical trust on DiabeVita, gold detailing for a heritage, premium feel on Shilajit Resin. All flagship packaging was designed personally, with junior designers supporting secondary creative execution.

Diabevita — the only redesign. It existed on paper when I joined. Rather than scrap it, I rebuilt it inside the new system. It's now a strong performer in offline distribution, with online sales growing month-on-month from a low base, and zero product complaints since relaunch.

The other six — built from zero. Every other launched product was designed from a blank canvas inside the system. Junior designers executed creative extensions and social variants; every premium, brand-defining piece was designed personally.

Print production is the part of the process most portfolios leave out. Traveling with the founder to printing companies across Delhi — including one of India's largest — meant negotiating specifications, materials, and finishes that fit the budget without compromising the premium feel. This is where design meets manufacturing reality.

4.2 — E-COMMERCE & AMAZON OPTIMIZATION

Ownership here extended beyond the design layer to end-to-end management of Ecowell's Amazon and Shopify presence. I designed and shipped 6 complete Amazon listing systems — 50+ listing images and 74+ Amazon A+ Content banners, optimized for both desktop and mobile.

Every listing was built as a conversion funnel, engineered to move a customer from attention to purchase through a deliberate sequence:

  • Hero imagery — stop the scroll, land the promise.
  • Benefit breakdowns — translate ingredients into outcomes.
  • Ingredient education — build credibility through transparency.
  • Comparison modules — position Ecowell against category defaults.
  • Trust-building content — certifications, formulations, brand story.

Amazon A+ Content isn't decoration — it's persuasion architecture. Every banner was designed to close the gap between a customer's need and a product's promise. Getting that right depended on studying consumer psychology, reading purchasing behavior, tracking market trends, and identifying the trust signals unique to each supplement category — then translating all of it into visuals that attract, educate, and convert within a single scroll.

4.3 — SOCIAL & DIGITAL

Built the digital content ecosystem from the ground up — 300+ static creatives, 10+ motion graphics, and a 2026 campaign spanning 150+ assets across 14 creative series — while also directly supporting marketing execution alongside the ground team. The Shopify D2C storefront, live for a year, carries the same system across product pages, category pages, and campaign banners, with merchandising and creative rollout managed end-to-end.

What this added up to wasn't just consistent files. It was the founder realizing Ecowell needed an actual brand, not a series of one-off product decisions — and it made me the person the company turns to for any design problem, from a single label spec to a full campaign rollout.

06

Reflection

The biggest lesson wasn't visual — it was realizing how far real-world constraints sit from imagination-driven design. Every assumption about what looks good had to be tested against a harder question: what does someone actually feel in the first two seconds of seeing this — do the trust signals read instantly, or only in theory? Getting the design right was only step one. After that, everything gets re-tested against marketing performance, ad response, and real customer behavior — and that loop never really stops.

What I'd Do Differently

If I were starting this project again, I'd front-load more rigorous market research before forming a design direction, rather than leaning on instinct and founder conviction alone. I'd also build the full packaging system — the constants and the category rules — before the first product launched, so every SKU after that inherited structure instead of being retrofitted into one. And I'd bring in more experienced collaborators, on both the design and marketing side, earlier in the process rather than growing the team reactively.
